Module 5 · Non-linearity

Why 95% busy isn’t “a bit more” than 80%

Turn the dial on a warehouse receiving gate and watch what happens to the queue — then make two calls a supply chain manager actually has to make.

The gate has 3 unloading docks, takes about 45 minutes per truck and runs from 06:00 to 16:00 — around 40 slots a day. The yard holds 10 trucks.

The takeaway

Below about 80% busy, extra trucks are nearly free — the gate absorbs them and the wait barely moves. Past 90%, every extra point buys less slack, and the same small increase costs far more than the last one.

At 99% the yard is full all day, deliveries roll into tomorrow, and one late truck is enough to break the schedule — because there is no slack left to absorb it.

Utilisation feels linear on a spreadsheet. The queue it creates is not.
